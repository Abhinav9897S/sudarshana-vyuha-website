From footage to floor plan.

Stage 1 of 5

01 · Footage

Any camera. Any video.

Phone, helmet cam, handheld or drone, in MP4, MOV, AVI or MKV. Several clips of one building fuse into one model, and every frame stays traceable to its camera.

49 / 49 frames fused from two operators

02 · Frames

Only sharp, unique frames survive.

Sampling adapts to the frame rate. Laplacian variance rejects blur, near-duplicates are pruned, and hard cuts in edited footage are found and never matched across.

7 of 7 hard cuts found in an 83 s tactical clip

03 · Poses

Where every camera stood.

Structure-from-Motion matches features across frames, solves each camera's position and orientation, and triangulates a sparse skeleton of the scene.

79 of 80 images registered · 93.6 s end to end

04 · Surface

Then every surface it saw.

PatchMatch stereo on the GPU fills the skeleton in, fuses it into a dense cloud and meshes it. Where no camera looked, the model keeps a gap.

674,094 points from 30 s of near-textureless snow

05 · Plan

A floor plan you can brief from.

The cloud is sliced, gridded and fitted with evidence-checked walls. Doorways stay open. Exported as SVG, PNG and JSON.

8.00 × 5.00 m room recovered as 8.06 × 5.00 m

The console

Four steps. Then press start.

Pick a mission, load the footage, choose how far to take it, and review. Every stage runs in the background while the log stays live. This is a working replica of the desktop app, replaying verified runs.

The one rule

Nothing is invented. Every stage may select, weight or discard what the cameras observed, but none may add to it. When a stage fails, you get a gap in the model, never a confident invention.

Network

Zero requests at runtime.

No cloud APIs, no telemetry, no web fonts. Install once, then disconnect the machine for good.

Hardware

Any modern PC.

Runs on an everyday Windows laptop or desktop. A dedicated GPU speeds up the heavy stages, but the app works without one.

Honesty

Gaps, not guesses.

Areas that cannot be joined are reported as separate. Scale from camera height is always labelled approximate.
